Gain Planner

Calorie targets and meal plans for adults who want to gain weight steadily. Made by Cinderspire.

Good to know: If you are underweight due to illness, pregnant, under 18, or have an eating disorder, talk to a doctor first. Gain Planner gives general estimates for adults and is not medical advice.

Questions

How is my calorie target worked out?

With the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for resting energy, multiplied by an activity factor, plus a mild (+250) or moderate (+500) daily surplus. Protein is 1.4, 1.6 or 2.0 grams per kilogram, fat is 30% of calories and carbohydrate fills the rest. It is an estimate: real needs differ by up to about 10%, so judge the plan by your weekly average over two to three weeks.

Where do the food numbers come from?

USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy), a public-domain database, bundled in the app. Portion sizes are typical household amounts and are shown next to each food. Gain Planner is not affiliated with any government body.

Does it work offline? Where is my data?

Yes. Everything is stored on your device. There is no account. You > Settings > Erase all my data deletes it.

Can I leave out foods?

Yes: choose vegetarian meals and leave out peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, eggs, gluten, fish, shellfish, soy or sesame in your details. Always check labels yourself if you have an allergy.

Why does my plan say I am a little over or under my target?

Portions move in steps (for example one egg or half a cup), so a day can land within a few percent of the target. Use the plus and minus buttons on any item to fine-tune.

What does the rate check mean?

After three weigh-ins spanning about ten days, the app shows your weekly trend and compares it with the usual 0.25 to 0.5 kg (about 0.5 to 1 lb) a week range. It is a guide, not a target you must hit.
